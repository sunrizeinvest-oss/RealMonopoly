## Part 2 — Valuation

### The number range

For a Canadian B2B SaaS pre-seed with live product + no revenue + clear wedge:

| Scenario | Post-money cap |
|---|---|
| Soft signals (no product, no team) | $2M-$3M |
| Live product, no users (you, today) | **$3M-$6M** |
| Live product + 1-2 design partners + LOI revenue | $5M-$8M |
| Live product + $5K-$20K MRR | $6M-$10M |
| Strong traction, seed-adjacent | $8M-$15M |

**Your target: $4M-$6M post-money cap on a SAFE.**

### How to set the number

The honest answer: **valuation is what an investor agrees to pay.** It's not formula-driven at this stage. But here's the framework:

**Step 1 — Set your ask amount.**
You said $500K. Use $500K as the round size.

**Step 2 — Set your acceptable dilution.**
At pre-seed, founders typically accept 5-10% dilution. More than 10% is a red flag (you'll be over-diluted before Series A).

**Step 3 — Work backwards.**
- 5% dilution + $500K raise = $10M post-money
- 10% dilution + $500K raise = $5M post-money

**Step 4 — Set the cap at the high end of your range.**
$4M-$5M post-money cap = 4-5% dilution = good range for pre-seed.

### What to say when asked

Investor: *"What valuation are you raising at?"*

You: *"We're raising $500K on a SAFE with a $5M post-money cap. Open to a conversation on terms if there's a strong lead."*

This is **specific, not negotiable-feeling, and signals you've done the work.** Vague answers ("whatever the market accepts") signal you haven't.

### Don't optimize for the highest cap

A $7M cap that takes 9 months to close is worse than a $5M cap that closes in 6 weeks. **Speed > 30% upside on dilution at pre-seed.** The seed round is where you reset valuation up significantly anyway.

## Part 3 — Instrument (SAFE vs alternatives)

### Use a SAFE. Period.

**Why:**
- Standard in 2026, no surprises for sophisticated investors
- Lawyer cost: ~$2K-$5K total (vs $25K-$75K for a priced round)
- Closing in days, not weeks
- No board seat triggered
- No security to register
- Y Combinator's template is free and battle-tested

**The four SAFE variants:**

| SAFE type | Valuation cap | Discount | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Post-money cap, no discount** | Yes | None | **Your default.** Cleanest math, easiest to negotiate. |
| Pre-money cap, no discount | Yes | None | Older standard. Avoid unless investor insists. |
| Cap + discount | Yes | 20% | Investor wants both protections. Rare at pre-seed. |
| MFN-only (no cap) | None | None | Bridge between rounds. Don't lead with this. |

**Your default:** Post-money cap, no discount, MFN clause included.

### What the MFN clause does

If you later sign a SAFE with better terms (lower cap, higher discount), all previous SAFE-holders automatically get those better terms. **This is investor-friendly but it's market standard at pre-seed.** Don't fight it.

### What NOT to do

- **Don't issue a convertible note.** Older instrument, has an interest rate, has a maturity date that can force conversion. SAFE is cleaner.
- **Don't do a priced equity round at $200K.** Legal cost eats too much, takes 2-3 months, requires a lead investor.
- **Don't accept revenue-based financing or debt at this stage.** Saves dilution but punishes growth rate.
- **Don't do SAFE with no cap AND no discount.** That's a "MFN-only" SAFE — it's a placeholder, not a real investment.

## Part 4 — Key terms you need to understand

### Cap table basics

**Pre-money valuation** = company value BEFORE the new investment.
**Post-money valuation** = company value AFTER the new investment.
**Post-money** = pre-money + new investment amount.

Example:
- Pre-money $4.5M + $500K investment = $5M post-money
- Investor owns $500K / $5M = 10% of the company

A "$5M post-money cap" SAFE means: when the SAFE converts (at your next priced round), the investor.s $500K converts at a $5M post-money valuation OR the next round's valuation, whichever is LOWER.

### The terms that matter at pre-seed

| Term | What it means | What's normal | What to fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Valuation cap** | Max price at which the SAFE converts | $3M-$8M for your stage | Cap above $8M (investor will say no) |
| **Discount** | Discount on next round price | 0% (modern) to 20% (older) | Discount above 20% |
| **MFN clause** | Get best terms of any future SAFE | Standard | Removing MFN entirely |
| **Pro-rata rights** | Right to maintain % in future rounds | Standard for $50K+ checks | Pro-rata for sub-$25K checks |
| **Information rights** | Quarterly financial updates | Standard | Monthly + audit rights at pre-seed |
| **Board seat** | Investor gets formal board representation | **Never at pre-seed** | Any pre-seed board seat ask |
| **Liquidation preference** | Order of payout on exit | 1x non-participating | 2x participating or higher |
| **Founder vesting** | Founders earn shares over time | 4-year vest, 1-year cliff | Aggressive acceleration clauses against you |

### Two terms you must NOT give up at pre-seed

1. **Board seat.** Pre-seed investors do not get a board seat. Period. Even if they offer $200K. The seed lead gets the first board seat, not pre-seed angels. If someone insists, walk.
2. **Founder common-stock control.** You should still own >50% of the common voting stock after this round. Doing simple SAFE math at $500K on $5M cap, you.ll be at ~90% post-round (excluding any prior friends/family). That's fine.

## Part 5 — Process timeline

### Realistic timeline from first outreach to wire

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Soft outreach. 30-50 LinkedIn DMs + 10 warm-intro emails. |
| 2 | First responses come in. 5-15 first meetings scheduled. |
| 3-4 | First meetings (30-60 min each). 80% no's, 20% follow-ups. |
| 5-6 | Second meetings with the follow-ups. Diligence questions emerge. |
| 7 | First soft commits ("I'm in for $25K when you're ready to close"). |
| 8-9 | Build momentum to a critical mass of $300K-$500K committed. |
| 10-11 | Send signed SAFE documents. Investors sign + wire. |
| 12 | Round closes. |

**Plan for 12 weeks.** It WILL take longer than you expect. Compressing it forces bad investors.

### The "rolling close" model

You don't need to close everyone on the same day. Modern SAFE rounds **close as money comes in.** Each investor signs their SAFE individually, wires the money, you bank it, and you keep raising. This is called a "rolling close."

**Why this matters:**
- First $25K-$50K of commitments create momentum for the rest.
- You can deposit money and start spending it before the round is "done."
- No artificial deadline pressure.

## Part 7 — The pitch meeting

### What 30-60 minutes looks like

| Minute | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | Brief intro: you, the company, the ask |
| 2-10 | The problem + the wedge |
| 10-25 | Live demo (X-Ray + LTL + IC memo) |
| 25-35 | Investor questions (let them drive) |
| 35-50 | Numbers: ask, valuation, use of funds, timeline |
| 50-60 | Next steps + close the meeting |

### The five questions you'll be asked

1. **"What's the moat?"** → The LTL parser nobody else ships at any price + 6-city Canadian open-data adapter set.
2. **"What's your traction?"** → Honest: "Live product, Stripe live mode, pre-revenue. Outbound starting this week. Goal: 100 paying users by month 6."
3. **"Why hasn't CoStar done this?"** → Slide 8 of your deck has the answer prepared.
4. **"How do you get to seed?"** → 100 paying users, $30K MRR, 1 named design partner case study, 6 months.
5. **"What's the exit?"** → Strategic acquisition by CoStar / Altus / Yardi (industry comp: CoStar has acquired 10+ companies in 5 years). Or Series A → growth round → IPO at $1B+ in the institutional adjacent space (LoopNet, Buildout examples).

### What NOT to do in a pitch meeting

- Don't promise things you can't deliver ("real-time MLS data, off-market sourcing")
- Don't say "we have no competitors" — say "here's our defensible position vs. CoStar"
- Don't say "I have other offers" if you don't
- Don't take longer than 60 minutes
- Don't pitch with slides if the investor wants the demo first — ask them what they want
- Don't send them an NDA before the meeting (sophisticated investors won't sign)

### What TO do after the pitch meeting

- Send a thank-you email within 24 hours with the deck + one-pager + Loom + sample IC memo
- Add them to your fundraise CRM (Notion / Airtable / spreadsheet)
- Set a reminder to follow up in 5-7 days if they don't respond
- Once: don't follow up more than that

## Part 10 — Canadian-specific

### SR&ED (huge)

Scientific Research & Experimental Development tax credit. **Refunds 35-65% of eligible R&D spending** (engineering salaries, contractor costs, prototyping). You're definitely eligible — the AI underwriting, LTL parser, year-built estimator all count as eligible R&D.

- **Worth: $25K-$100K per year for a small startup**
- File: T661 form with your T2 corporate tax return
- Hire a SR&ED specialist (not your accountant). They take 15-25% on a contingency basis but get you the money fast.

### CDAP / IRAP

| Program | What | How much |
|---|---|---|
| CDAP (Canada Digital Adoption Program) | Digital transformation grant | Up to $15K |
| IRAP (Industrial Research Assistance) | NRC-backed R&D funding | $50K-$500K |

IRAP is incredibly competitive but worth applying. Apply in parallel with VC raise.

### BDC

Business Development Bank of Canada — government-backed lender + small VC arm.
- BDC Capital invests in pre-seed → seed → growth
- More patient than commercial VCs
- Less brand value than top-tier VCs
- Apply only after you have at least one commercial investor signal

### Corporate structure

**Recommendation:** Federal corporation (CCPC — Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation).
- Capital gains exemption ($971K lifetime, indexed)
- Small business deduction (lower tax on first $500K)
- Eligible for SR&ED at maximum credit rate

**Don't:**
- Don't incorporate in Delaware (Canadian founder + Canadian customers = tax inefficient)
- Don't operate as a sole proprietorship (no liability protection, no eligibility for grants)

If you haven't incorporated yet: do it BEFORE raising. Investors won't fund an unincorporated entity. Talk to a startup-focused lawyer like Mike Stephenson at LawScope or any Borden Ladner Gervais associate. ~$1,500-$3,000 all-in.

## Part 14 — Closing advice

### The single most important thing

**Optimize for the right investors, not the highest valuation.**

A $4M cap from a family office that opens 5 doors > a $6M cap from a random angel who pays cash and disappears.

The best pre-seed investors are:
1. Founders who've exited (they bring pattern matching)
2. Strategic operators who become customers
3. Family offices that have RE expertise (Corey is this)
4. Pre-seed funds with strong portfolios
5. Last: cash-only angels

### Time allocation during the raise

- 50% — outreach + meetings (the actual raise)
- 30% — building (so you have something new to show in week 8)
- 20% — diligence prep + follow-up

Most founders flip this and spend 80% on building. **You will lose the round if you don't aggressively allocate time to outreach.**

### The "no" file

Track every no. Most of them will reverse course 3-12 months later when you have traction. A "soft no" today is a "yes" at seed if you keep them on the monthly investor update.

### Monthly investor updates

Even before you close anyone, send a monthly update to everyone you've spoken to:
- Wins of the month (paying customers, hires, product ships)
- Numbers (MRR, signups, churn)
- Ask (intros, specific help)
- Burn rate / runway

**This is how cold leads become warm leads.** Founders who do this raise 2-3x faster than those who don't.
